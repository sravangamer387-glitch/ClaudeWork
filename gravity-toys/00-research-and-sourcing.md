## 4. Compliance (India first)

- **Toys QCO** – toys sold in India (made or imported) must conform to BIS standards and carry the
  **ISI mark**; manufacturers need a BIS licence (factory assessment + testing at a BIS-recognised lab). [S]
- **IS 9873 (Part 1):2025** (mechanical/physical) now runs concurrently with the 2019 edition until
  **22 Mar 2027** (circular 30 Jan 2026) → design to the 2025 edition. **Product Manual PM/9873/13**
  (Jan 2026) covers Parts 1,2,3,4,7,9,10,11 and IS 15644 for electrical toys. [S – consultancy sources; confirm on bis.gov.in]
- Part 3 = migration of certain elements (paint/lacquer/weights) → relevant to every painted wood toy.
- Export: EN 71 (EU), ASTM F963 + CPSIA (US), ISO 8124. Small parts/small-ball/magnet clauses drive
  our design rules below.
- **Design rules adopted across all 5 toys** (verify numerically against the standards):
  1. Age grade ≥ 4 unless noted; every loose ball is a small-part hazard under age 3 → age label + warning.
  2. No loose magnets, no button cells.
  3. Edge radius ≥ 2 mm, no pinch gaps 5–12 mm accessible to fingers (check finger-entrapment clauses).
  4. No lead; heavy-metal test on every paint, lacquer, weight.
  5. Wood moisture 8–10 %, sanded ≤ 180 grit, no splinters.

Labs: **Bharat Test House** (NABL + BIS-recognised, toys) [S] · **Krishna Metallurgical Laboratories**
(NABL, IS 9873 / IS 15644) [S] · **Intertek Bengaluru** (ISO 17025 toy lab) [S]. Locations of the first
two not confirmed – check the BIS recognised-lab list and NABL scope.

## 5. Cost assumptions used in the toy files (to be replaced by RFQs)
- Hyderabad laser cutting: ₹ 3–6 per minute machine time + material.
- Hand-turned wooden part: ₹ 8–40 depending on complexity.
- Small injection mould (2–4 cavity, P20 steel): ₹ 6–20 lakh; large multi-part family mould: ₹ 20 lakh+.
- BIS licence + lab testing per product family: budget ₹ 1.5–4 lakh and 3–5 months.
- Retail = ~3.5–4× landed ex-factory cost (distributor + retailer margins).
