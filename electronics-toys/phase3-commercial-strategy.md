# Phase 3 · Commercial Strategy

All numbers are from `top50.csv` and my assumptions; they must be replaced with real quotes.

## 1. The core problem
At 1,000-unit costs, most electronic toys earn only **about 6–22 % contribution on a marketplace** (after 18 % GST, fees, ads and returns). A few lose money (LED fidget spinner –₹2 per unit). A stand-alone ₹799–1,299 toy cannot carry fixed costs for BIS testing, moulds, content and ads.

## 2. Five levers (use all of them)
1. **Shared platforms** – 3 boards reused across many toys:
   - **Audio board** (MCU or voice IC + speaker + LEDs + buttons): rhymes player, learning tablet, flashcard reader, voice clock, abacus, star projector, cricket game, doctor kit, lullaby soother, quiz buzzer. One firmware base, one PCB family, one BIS family approval where rules allow.
   - **Motor board** (driver + sensors): bump-and-go vehicles, dancing robot, obstacle car, line follower, train.
   - **STEM kit board** (MCU + sockets): snap lab, coding kit, weather station.
   Benefit: fewer PCB designs, lower MOQ per part, faster approvals, shared test jigs.
2. **Volume discount on parts** – target 5,000–10,000 units per board family; BOM falls (assumption 15–25 %) as MOQs rise. Plan reorders on the same board.
3. **Content moat** – regional-language audio library (Hindi, Telugu, Tamil, Kannada, Malayalam, Marathi, Bengali) recorded with your own voice talent so you own the rights. Import competitors cannot match this cheaply. Use only public-domain or licensed rhymes and music. Content is reusable across toys.
4. **Bundles and boxes** – "Learning Box" (tablet + flashcards + clock) at ₹2,499; festival gift sets; school sets. Bundles raise average order value and spread fixed shipping cost.
5. **Channel mix** – marketplaces for discovery and reviews; **own site and schools/pre-schools/B2B** for margin; gifting for Diwali/Christmas; export of the Indian-language line to diaspora markets later (needs ASTM/EN/CPSIA testing).

## 4. Price and margin guide (marketplace, before lever effects)
- Do not list below ₹799 unless the toy is an add-on or bundle component.
- Target **≥ 25 % contribution** before launching a product; if below 15 %, redesign (cheaper enclosure, fewer features) or reprice.
- Items above ₹1,000 pay a placeholder 8 % referral; keep an eye on Amazon's toys fee page.
- Hold back stock until compliance clears; electronics have higher return rates (assumed 8 % of landed cost).

## 6. Financial frame for Wave 1 (rough)
- Development per platform board (design, firmware, prototypes, tooling): ₹3–6 lakh each *(assumption)*.
- IS 15644 + IS 9873 testing and BIS licence per product family: ₹2–4 lakh *(assumption)*.
- Content recording and licensing: ₹1–2 lakh *(assumption)*.
- Pilot stock 500–1,000 units per product: ₹2–6 lakh each.
- Realistic Wave 1 capital for 3 launch products: **about ₹20–30 lakh** *(assumption)*. Start with 3 products, not 10.

## 7. Risks
| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Thin marketplace margin | Levers 1–5; kill products under 15 % |
| Cheaper imports after the 2025 duty cut | Compete on local language, warranty, curriculum fit |
| Firmware/hardware defects | Burn-in test jig, AOI, golden-sample approval |
| Compliance delay | Platform family certification; no radio/lithium in Wave 1 |
| Content copyright | Own recordings; written talent release; licensed music only |
| Child data/privacy | No cameras, accounts, or apps in Wave 1 |
| Returns and warranty | 6-month replacement; spare-parts stock; clear manuals |
